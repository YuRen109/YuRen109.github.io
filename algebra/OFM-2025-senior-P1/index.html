What We Learned

  1. Boundedness and Monotonicity of Integer Sequences: A bounded monotone sequence of integers must eventually become constant.
    Consequently, a bounded sequence of integers that is not eventually constant must have infinitely many increasing pairs of consecutive terms and infinitely many decreasing pairs of consecutive terms.

  2. Discreteness of Integers: If and are integers satisfying , then .
    This property allows us to obtain a uniform lower bound on the difference between two distinct integer terms.

  3. Accumulation of Differences: Even if the difference between two individual terms is small, selecting sufficiently many pairs of terms with differences of the same sign may produce an arbitrarily large difference between their sums.
    In this problem, the accumulated difference eventually exceeds the upper bound of an individual term, leading to a contradiction.
