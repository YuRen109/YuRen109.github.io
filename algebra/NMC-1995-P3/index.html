What We Learned

  1. Proof by contradiction: Establishing incompatible upper and lower bounds can be an effective way to prove inequalities.

  2. Partitioning variables: Dividing variables into groups according to a suitable threshold can help exploit their different contributions to a sum or a sum of squares.

  3. Equality cases: When determining equality conditions, it is important to distinguish strict inequalities from non-strict inequalities and identify when all relevant bounds can be attained simultaneously.

  4. Using constraints together: Conditions on the sum and the sum of squares can provide stronger restrictions when considered simultaneously rather than separately.
