What We Learned

  1. Cancellation of Overlapping Regions: When comparing the sums of two overlapping regions, the contributions from their common entries cancel out.
    In particular, shifting an subgrid by one unit changes only entries being removed and entries being added. This allows us to establish a bound on the difference between the two subgrid sums without calculating the sums individually.

  2. From Local Conditions to Global Properties: A restriction on the difference between two adjacent objects may impose a global restriction on an entire collection of objects, provided that any two objects can be connected through a sequence of adjacent objects.
    In this problem, the bound on the difference between adjacent subgrid sums, together with the assumption that every subgrid sum has an absolute value greater than , forces all subgrid sums to have the same sign.

  3. Partitioning and Global Constraints: Partitioning an object into pairwise disjoint smaller objects allows us to express a global quantity as the sum of several local quantities.
    In this problem, partitioning the entire grid into four subgrids connects the restrictions on individual subgrid sums to the balanced condition of the entire grid.
